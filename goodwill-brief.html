From manual reporting to management visibility

Presented by Debie Coble, President & CEO Partner judge: Amanda Baumer Demo: Sun 4:30 PM · Pod B · Room 154 Code freeze: Sun 4:00 PM

“A monthly E-Commerce dashboard should balance growth, profitability, productivity, inventory management and customer engagement.”

“A nightly report creates a daily pulse. Each nightly report should show both revenue and customer count by marketplace, followed by enterprise totals.”

“From manual month-end close to Business Central integration. The current close combines portal downloads, emailed reports, bank activity, spreadsheet rules and manual Business Central entries.”

Goodwill Michiana, slides 19–42 of the kickoff deck. Open the demo with these words.

Goodwill Michiana sells donated goods online through several marketplaces. Every number leadership sees today is assembled by a person: logging into portals, downloading reports, counting spreadsheet rows, and copying totals into a workbook that is then pasted into their accounting system, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central. They are asking for three things, at three different speeds.

Daily

Nightly pulse report

Revenue and customer count for each marketplace (ShopGoodwill, Amazon, eBay, other), then enterprise totals. Delivered every night with nobody touching it.

Monthly

E-commerce dashboard and COO scorecard

15 KPIs on one page across growth, profitability, productivity, inventory and engagement, tracking the three 2027 plan anchors.

Month-end

Close automation into Business Central

Nine source reports go through allocation rules and become balanced General Journal lines and an AR invoice in Business Central, with reconciliation evidence.

The key insight for our team: all three asks sit on one shared foundation. Pull every source into one place, clean it into one common order format, and keep the raw files. The nightly report, the dashboard and the month-end close are then three different views of the same data. Build the foundation once and every demo gets stronger.
Current state

How it's done today

The slides walk through the manual steps with screenshots. Knowing these exactly is what earns points for “Fits Their Constraints”: it shows we understood the job below the surface.

Every day: the Daily Summary Spreadsheet

Upright portal (6 steps)
  1. Open Reports from the Upright home dashboard
  2. Under Downloads, click Paid orders
  3. Set the date range
  4. Generate the report
  5. Download it (Excel)
  6. Customer count = rows minus the title row, then type the numbers into the Daily Summary Spreadsheet
Cash Monkey, for Goodwill Books (4 steps)
  1. Open Cash Monkey reports
  2. Orders Report
  3. Pick dates from the drop-down
  4. Click the link and the report downloads
The step-6 screenshot shows a =SUM() totals row at the bottom of the export. If our parser counts rows naively, it overcounts customers by one. Handling this is a good “messy input” to show in the demo.

Every month: the close

Gather 9 sources, then run the allocation workbook (6 steps)
  1. Archive inputs under Accounting / Month End / year / month / Journal Entries / E-Commerce JEs
  2. Roll the workbook: open last month's E-Commerce Allocation file and save a copy for this month
  3. Populate tabs: type report data into the orange-highlighted fields on each source tab
  4. Generate entries: workbook formulas flow the data into Journal Entry tabs
  5. Post General Journal: copy and paste the journal output into a Business Central General Journal
  6. Create AR invoice in Business Central from the Invoices tab
The workbook is the real “control layer”. The business rules live in its formulas and in people's heads. Goodwill says the target is to automate the rules, not only the downloads.

Breakdown

Features F1–F8

Each card covers what the feature is, why Goodwill needs it, what goes in and comes out, where it gets hard, and what “done” looks like for this weekend. The tag shows how important it is for our demo.

F1

Source intake

Must

Get each report into the system without a person clicking through a portal. This is Goodwill's Workstream 1, “Reliable monthly source package”, and it also feeds the nightly report.

Inputs
  • Portal exports (CSV / Excel) from Upright, Cash Monkey, ShopGoodwill, eBay, Amazon
  • Email attachments (Goodwill Books statement, Upright's emailed report)
  • Bank activity (shipping) and FedEx
Outputs
  • Raw files, one per source per date range, handed to F2
  • A status per source: received, missing, or failed
Three ways to acquire, easiest first
  • Watched folder (OneDrive or SharePoint): staff drop the export and the pipeline does the rest. Works this weekend.
  • Email pickup: read attachments from a shared mailbox, for example with Power Automate (Microsoft 365).
  • Direct API: eBay and Amazon have seller APIs. For Upright, ShopGoodwill and Cash Monkey we need to confirm what access exists.
Hard parts
  • Credentials: we don't have Goodwill's logins, and we must not ask for real ones. Use sample files in the real format.
  • Each portal has different timing (Amazon reports must be requested and refreshed first).
  • Detecting that a report should have arrived and didn't.
Done this weekend: dropping the sample exports into a folder triggers ingestion for at least 3 sources, and a missing source is reported as missing, not silently zero.
F2

Archive and run history

Must (small)

Save every raw input exactly as received, under the folder convention Goodwill already uses, and log every run. Accountants and auditors need to trace any posted number back to the file it came from.

Behavior
  • Path: Month End/2026/09/Journal Entries/E-Commerce JEs/<source>_<period>.xlsx
  • Raw files are never edited; transformations write new files.
  • Run log: when, which files, row counts, totals, who approved.
Why it matters
  • It's step 01 of their manual process and stage 02 of their target close.
  • It's cheap to build and makes the reconciliation story (F8) credible.
Done this weekend: after a run, the archive folder and a run-log row exist, and the demo can click from a total to its source file.
F3

Normalize and enrich

Must

Every marketplace exports different columns. Convert them all into one common order table so the downstream features never care where an order came from. Goodwill's “Enrich” stage also adds supplier, source label and period.

Suggested common schema
  • channel, order_id, order_date, buyer_id
  • item_count, subtotal, shipping, tax, fees, refund, total
  • category, supplier / store, payment_type
  • source_file, source_row, period (for traceability)
Cleaning rules we already know
  • Drop the title row and the totals row before counting.
  • Upright exports carry Upright order ID, channel, channel order ID, buyer, item count, payment type, total, subtotal, shipping.
  • Jewelry: fill in Supplier (done today with a pivot step the slides call “Co-Pivot”).
  • Normalize dates and time zones so “the day” means the same thing in every source.
Done this weekend: 3+ differently shaped sample files land in one table with identical columns, with a test that proves the totals row is excluded.

F5

Monthly dashboard and COO scorecard

Should

One page, monthly, with 15 KPIs grouped into Financial, Productivity, Inventory, Sales, and Category + Customer, with “trend and target context … as data becomes available”. The 2027 plan anchors are net margin, revenue per labor hour and sell-through rate, all going up every year. Those three should be the most prominent things on the page.

What makes it hard
  • Sales data alone covers only some KPIs. Margin needs costs, revenue per labor hour needs labor hours, and days-to-list needs donation and listing dates. See the KPI table.
  • Year-over-year needs last year's data.
How to handle it honestly
  • Compute what sales data supports. For labor hours and costs, add a small monthly input sheet (it's how they'd start in real life too).
  • Mark each KPI as live, manual input, or not yet available. Judges reward saying what's simulated.
  • If they already pay for Power BI, a Power BI-ready dataset is a strong fit.
Done this weekend: the three 2027 anchors plus 5–8 more KPIs render from the same normalized data used by F4.
F6

Rules and mapping (the workbook, as code)

Must for the close

This is the hard core of the month-end ask and Goodwill's Workstream 3. The allocation workbook's formulas decide which amounts go to which GL account and department. We rebuild that logic as readable, versioned rules that finance staff can review.

Known rules from the slides
  • Monthly date range per source; Cash Monkey and Upright pull the full month.
  • ShopGoodwill: Period 1 = periodic reports only; Period 3 = all reports.
  • Shipping (OSM / PB / EasyPost): from 1st Source account 0101 → GL 10009.
  • FedEx: GL 40356, Dept 180, vendor V00122, net out BNKDEPOSIT refunds.
  • Jewelry: Supplier assignment.
Build approach
  • Rules as a config file (YAML / JSON / a sheet): source → account, department, debit or credit, filter.
  • Each rule has a test with a known input and expected journal lines.
  • Unknown rules: we don't have their workbook, so we implement the documented ones and design the format so the rest slot in. Say this in the demo.
Done this weekend: the FedEx and shipping rules run against sample data and produce correct, balanced lines, with a test suite proving it.
F7

Business Central output

Should

Replace copy-paste into Business Central with ready-to-import entries: General Journal lines (step 05 today) and an AR (accounts receivable) sales invoice (step 06). Workstream 4 asks to “capture import/API validation and posting response”.

Two levels
  • Level 1, file: generate an Excel or CSV in the column layout BC's General Journal import accepts (posting date, document no., account type, account no., description, amount, department dimension). Low risk, works this weekend.
  • Level 2, API: Business Central has a REST API with endpoints for journals, journal lines and sales invoices. A free BC sandbox trial could show a real post. Stretch goal.
Must be true of every output
  • Balanced: debits = credits per document.
  • Traceable: each line links back to its source file and row (F2).
  • Never auto-posted without approval until proven against the workbook. That is Goodwill's own rollout plan.
Done this weekend: a balanced General Journal import file and an AR invoice payload are generated from the sample month, and you can show the file opening in the expected format.
F8

Close controls and reconciliation

Must (it's the differentiator)

Goodwill's control principle: “Business Central receives balanced, traceable journal and invoice payloads; missing reports, failed rules and posting errors remain visible for review.” Most teams will skip this. Building it shows we understood that finance teams care more about trust than speed.

Checks
  • Every required source for the month is present (9 of 9).
  • Source total = normalized total = journal total = posted total.
  • Each journal document balances.
  • Row counts match (no dropped rows, totals row excluded exactly once).
Exception handling
  • An exception list: what failed, which source, who owns it.
  • Approval step before posting.
  • A one-page close report stored with the archive.
Done this weekend: feed it a broken month (one source missing, one refund) and the demo shows the exceptions caught and explained.

Recommendation

What we build this weekend

Ground rule 06: a small thing that works beats a big thing that does not. Goodwill's own rollout plan says to automate source by source and prove each one against the workbook. Our demo should do the same thing in miniature: one thin, real slice through the whole system rather than eight half-features.

Core demo

One pipeline, two outputs

  • Realistic sample exports for Upright, eBay, Amazon, ShopGoodwill (including the totals row and a refund)
  • F1 folder drop → F2 archive → F3 normalized table
  • F4 nightly pulse, generated and sent
  • F6 + F7 for 2–3 sources (FedEx, shipping, one marketplace) → balanced General Journal import file
  • F8 reconciliation page that catches a missing source
If time allows

Visibility layer

  • F5 scorecard: the 3 anchors plus ASP, revenue, repeat buyers, top categories
  • Monthly input sheet for labor hours and costs
  • AR invoice payload
  • Email-attachment pickup for Goodwill Books
Stretch

Real integrations

  • Post to a Business Central sandbox through its API and show the posting response
  • Pull from the eBay or Amazon seller APIs (sandbox)
  • Power Automate or Power BI version, if Goodwill confirms they license it
The constraint judges will check: the rubric's example for Goodwill is “tools they already pay for”. They run Business Central, so they are almost certainly on Microsoft 365 (Excel, Outlook, SharePoint/OneDrive, maybe Power Automate and Power BI). Outputs should arrive as Excel files, emails or Teams messages, and imports into Business Central. Avoid asking them to adopt a new paid SaaS. A Python script or small app is fine as the engine, but its inputs and outputs should live in tools they already own.

Discovery

Questions to ask Goodwill

Goodwill itself says discovery must confirm credentials, report availability, Business Central destinations and existing workbook formulas. Asking these also scores on “did they engage the partner”.

  1. For the nightly report, is a “customer” an order or a unique buyer? Same question per channel.
  2. Which Microsoft 365 tools do you already license: Power Automate, Power BI, SharePoint, Teams?
  3. Where should the nightly report land: email, Teams, a shared Excel file?
  4. Can you share a redacted export from Upright and from one marketplace so our parsers match the real columns?
  5. Can you share a redacted copy (or just the tab list and formulas) of the E-Commerce Allocation workbook?
  6. Which Business Central import do you use today for the General Journal: a journal template, a configuration package, or manual entry?
  7. What is “Co-Pivot” in the Jewelry step, and how is Supplier decided?
  8. ShopGoodwill Period 1 vs Period 3: what are the periods, and why do they take different reports?
  9. Which marketplaces count as “other e-commerce channels” today?
  10. Where would labor hours and costs come from for revenue per labor hour and net margin?
  11. Does Upright record when an item was received or donated, for days from donation to listing?
  12. Who approves journal entries before posting, and what would make you trust an automated one?
  13. Which source is most painful or most error-prone today? We'll prioritize it.
Vocabulary

Glossary

Business Central (BC)
Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, Goodwill's accounting / ERP system. Everything in the close ends up here.
Month-end close
The process of recording all of a month's activity in the books so the month's financial statements are final.
General Journal (GJ)
The BC screen where accountants enter journal lines: account, amount, debit or credit, date, dimensions. Today the workbook output is pasted here.
Journal entry (JE)
A set of lines whose debits equal credits. “E-Commerce JEs” is the folder for these.
AR invoice
Accounts receivable invoice: a record that money is owed to Goodwill (for example by a marketplace that pays out later).
GL account
General ledger account number, such as 10009 or 40356. It decides where an amount lands in the books.
Dimension / Dept
An extra tag on a BC line for reporting, such as Department 180.
Vendor V00122
The BC vendor record used for FedEx charges.
BNKDEPOSIT
Bank deposit lines; FedEx refunds arrive this way and must be netted against charges.
Allocation workbook
The Excel file, copied forward each month, whose formulas turn source totals into journal and invoice entries. Orange cells are manual inputs.
Upright
The operations / listing portal shown in the slides (manifests, listings, paid orders, per-user production stats).
Cash Monkey
The platform behind the Goodwill Books channel; its Orders Report is pulled daily and monthly.
ShopGoodwill
Goodwill's own online auction marketplace.
Sell-through rate
Share of listed items that actually sold in a period. A 2027 anchor KPI.
ASP
Average selling price: revenue ÷ units sold.
Reconciliation
Proving two sets of numbers agree, for example that source reports total the same as what was posted.
